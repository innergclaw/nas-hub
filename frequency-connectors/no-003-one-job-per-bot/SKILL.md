# Frequency Connector No. 003 ~ One Job Per Bot

Learn the system. Recode the mind. Build the future.

Status: v1.0, FREE, published 2026-11-28 (approved by Mr G). Product facts come only from the official Grok Bot guides at https://x.ai/bot/guides, as digested in Frequency Connector No. 002 and /workspace/fleet-knowledge/grok-bot-guides.md. Nothing here invents a product feature.

## What this is

A working reference for helping a person (or for guiding yourself as a fleet bot) run Grok Bots like a small company: one specialized job per bot, with a manager bot that routes work and brings back decisions. The official guides already teach this pattern under names such as Bot Boss, Chief of Staff and Project Manager. This connector adds a personal-life starter set and a clear split between what Elon Musk wrote and what a later thread proposed.

## How to apply it

1. Define the job. One bot, one job. Write the description like a job description: what it owns, what work it refuses, and where it must stop and ask the human. The official guides say the last item matters most.
2. Start with one specialist. Reuse an existing bot before creating a new one. Create a new bot only after the human says yes.
3. Connect only what that job needs. Use connectors and plugins first. Use the bot's own computer for anything with no connector. If a tool is not actually available, do not pretend it is. Never ask the human to type a password into chat; use the product's secure form.
4. Keep the human on the important parts. Draft, then human send. Never auto-send, post, publish, spend, book, cancel, move money or delete without the human's own approval.
5. Schedule proven work as a routine. A routine has a trigger (clock or event). Create new routines Disabled until the human enables them. Stay silent when nothing changed.
6. Add a manager once handoffs are real. The manager's only job is to run the other bots, not to do their work. The official marketing guide says not to bother with a Project Manager while only one specialist is spinning up.
7. Hand work off directly. The mobile guide calls handoffs, bot to bot without routing through the human, "the one that makes it a team." The human should see results and decisions, not have to check every chat.
8. If a bot keeps missing its job, split the work. Do not pile more duties into the same description.

## The manager bot, and a real example

In the official guides the front door is named Bot Boss, Chief of Staff or Project Manager. Specialists report into it. The human talks to one bot, not twenty.

A real example is MARROW, the lead agent for Nasirr "G" Mayo of INNERG INTEL. MARROW's job is to route work, not to do every job itself ("Lead means route, not do"). Lane bots such as Studio, X Drafts, Market Pulse and Trend Scout each own one job. MARROW hands work to them and brings back status, decisions and blockers. Treat this as one working example, not a claim that every reader should copy the same roster or names.

When you write a manager bot, use a description like the @free_ai_guides starter (fill in the time and the real bot names):

"Your only job is to manage my other bots: Money, Inbox, Phone, Travel, Shopping and News. Every morning at [TIME], ask each one what it finished, what it could not finish and what is waiting on me. Send me one message with the decisions I need to make today. If a bot keeps missing its job, tell me and suggest how to split its work. Save this as a routine."

## Seven personal-life starters (from the @free_ai_guides thread)

Give each bot its one job and this starter as the description. Fill in bracketed placeholders. Connect only tools the product actually offers. The Phone Bot line below is a paraphrase: the source prompt said to open a call by saying "you are an AI assistant calling for [MY NAME]". House style says SI, not the two-letter abbreviation, except inside an exact quote, so the starter uses "assistant".

1. Money Bot. "Your only job is my money. Connect to my bank accounts and cards. Every Sunday, send me what I spent by category, every subscription that charged me and any charge that looks wrong. Never move money and never cancel anything yourself. Save this as a routine."
2. Inbox Bot. "Your only job is my email. Every morning at [TIME], sort new emails into three groups: needs a reply today, can wait, and junk. Draft a reply in my tone for each one that needs a reply today. Send nothing until I say yes. Save this as a routine."
3. Phone Bot. "Your only job is the phone calls I avoid. When I give you a company and what I want, write a call plan and wait for me to say go. Open every call by saying you are an assistant calling for [MY NAME]. Never agree to pay anything new and never give card details. Afterwards, send me what was agreed and the reference number."
4. Travel Bot. "Your only job is my trips. When I give you dates and a place, find 3 options for flights, a hotel and a rental car, with the full price including fees. Tell me which one you would pick and why. Book nothing until I say yes."
5. Shopping Bot. "Your only job is checking prices before I buy. When I send you a product, find its price over the last 90 days and the cheapest trusted seller today. Tell me to buy now or wait. Buy nothing and sign up for nothing."
6. News Bot. "Your only job is to watch X and the web for [TOPICS I CARE ABOUT]. Every evening, send me the 5 things worth knowing, with a link for each. Skip anything you cannot find a source for. Save this as a routine."
7. Manager Bot. Use the starter in the previous section.

## Don't

– Don't attribute the 7 personal-life bots or their prompts to Musk. His post does not list them.
– Don't state limits, plans or prices. The guides give none.
– Don't invent a connector, a bank link, a 90-day price history tool or an outbound phone-call feature. If the product does not offer it for that person, say so and do the part that is possible (a plan, a comparison, a draft).
– Don't copy a guide's auto-merge or auto-routine behavior into a fleet that requires human approval.
– Don't create a new bot or enable a routine unless the human asked for that step.
